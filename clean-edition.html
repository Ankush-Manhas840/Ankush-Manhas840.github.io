Real-Time Fraud Detection

A small version of a payment company's fraud pipeline, built on IBM TabFormer: 24.4 million card transactions from 2,000 people, about 12 frauds in every 10,000. The hard part is not the model. It is keeping the future out of the training data.

Real frauds in the top 1,000 alerts
Random guessing~1
First model142
Final model177

Logistic regression on five per-card features. The result held up on new people in a later period.

Drift I caught: online fraud per 10,000
Before April 201766.4
After April 20170.0

A feature that looked strong made the model worse. I confirmed the drift in a second group of people and left the feature out.

Dropped a dataset that leaked the label

In PaySim, balance columns were never updated for fraud rows, so they gave the answer away. Senders also never repeat, so per-card history was impossible. I switched datasets.

Split by time, not at random

Training data ends in April 2017 and validation starts after it, so the model is always judged on the future, the way it would be used.

Crop Yield Prediction

Predicts yield for rice, wheat, maize and sugarcane from Indian government data (246,091 raw rows, later updated to the official 1997–2022 series).

Test error (MAE, tonnes per hectare) — lower is better
Linear baseline107.63
Random forest11.91
Outliers removed2.04

Caught a silent unit bug

Checking quantiles showed a 175× jump between median and third quartile: some crops were recorded in nuts or bales, not tonnes. I kept only crops with consistent units. Breaking error down by crop then found one crop giving 65.15 against 0.55 for the rest.

Used Car Price Prediction

Resale price for everyday cars from about 15,400 CarDekho listings. Test R² 0.94, checked with 5-fold cross-validation (0.90 to 0.95 on every fold).

Test R² — higher is better
Random forest, all cars0.72
Linear baseline0.79
Everyday cars only0.94

One car broke the model

Ranking the biggest errors found a single ₹3.95 crore supercar, the only one of its brand. Removing brands with fewer than 10 listings (9 of 15,407 cars) took R² from 0.72 to 0.94.

How I work

A good score is where I start asking questions.

The same four habits run through every project above.

01

Baseline first

A simple model sets the bar, so every improvement means something.

02

Hunt for leakage

Check where the future or the answer can sneak into training data, and split by time.

03

Break the score apart

Error by crop, by brand, by period. The average hides where a model is wrong.

04

Write it down

What I ran, on what data, what came out, and what the model cannot do.
