Finding 01

Cut a strong feature

Adding "was the chip used" dropped real frauds in the top 1,000 alerts from 150 to 17. Online fraud fell from 66.4 to 0.0 per 10,000 after April 2017. The pattern changed, so the feature went.

Finding 02

Caught a leaking dataset

In PaySim, fraud rows were cancelled, so the receiver's balance never changed: 49.8% against 0.06% for normal payments. I switched to IBM TabFormer, 24.4 million rows.

Finding 03

Live matches training

Replaying one card's 5,011 purchases through the live Redis code gave the training features back. A live replay then caught 33 of 78 frauds, matching the offline result.

Finding 04

Fixed real failures

The machine ran out of network ports, and a purchase replayed twice dropped a caught fraud from 0.987 to 0.929. Both fixed, the second by making replays safe to repeat.

Method

流儀Three rules
i.

Measure first

Before I believe a model, I write down what a good result looks like and build the test for it. For FinSight that was 24 answers checked by hand against the printed page.

ii.

Remove what hurts

If the evidence says a feature or a dataset is making things worse, it goes, even when it looked strong. I confirm it on a second group of people before deciding.

iii.

Run it for real

A notebook score is not a system. I put it in a container, deploy it, replay real traffic through it, and check the live numbers match the offline ones.
