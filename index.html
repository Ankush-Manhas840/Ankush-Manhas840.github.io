Crop Yield Prediction

Live on Streamlit

Predicts yield for rice, wheat, maize and sugarcane from state, season, year and planted area — trained on public Indian government agricultural data.

Caught a silent unit bug

Per-quantile distributions showed a 175× jump from median to Q3: some crops were logged in nuts or bales, not tonnes. I scoped to crops with consistent units instead of guessing conversions.

Found where the error lived

Breaking MAE down by crop: one contributed 65.15 against 0.55 for the rest. Implausible outlier rows — removing them cut error 83%.

Honest about limits

Six documented limitations, including that a random forest can't extrapolate to future years — so it isn't sold as a forecaster.

One car broke the model

Ranking the largest errors exposed a single ₹3.95 Cr supercar — the only one of its brand. Dropping brands with under 10 listings (9 of 15,407 cars) took R² from 0.72 to 0.94.

Method

A good score isn't the finish line. It's where I start asking questions.

Baseline first

A weak model sets the bar, so every improvement means something.

Break the score apart

Error by crop, by brand — the average hides where it's actually wrong.

Validate properly

Held-out tests and k-fold cross-validation, not one lucky split.

Write down the limits

Every project says where its data came from and what it can't do.
